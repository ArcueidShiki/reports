# Investing Wisdom

> Bullet point · Author · Source

---

## Mindset & Philosophy

- "Investment rule #1: Never lose money. Rule #2: Never forget rule #1." · Warren Buffett · Berkshire Hathaway Shareholder Letters
- "In times of greed, be cautious; in times of caution, be greedy." · Warren Buffett · Berkshire Hathaway Shareholder Letters
- "You should invest in a business that a fool can operate, because someday a fool will." · Warren Buffett · Berkshire Hathaway Shareholder Letters
- "Life is like rolling a snowball; the important thing is finding very wet snow and a very long slope." · Warren Buffett · Berkshire Hathaway Shareholder Letters (compounding metaphor)
- Focus only on companies within your circle of competence; never invest outside it. · Warren Buffett · Berkshire Hathaway Shareholder Letters

## Value & Margin of Safety

- Aim to purchase $50 assets for $1; margin of safety is the cornerstone of conservative investing. · Benjamin Graham · *The Intelligent Investor*
- Internal value doesn't depend on market price fluctuations — price is what you pay, value is what you get. · Benjamin Graham · *Security Analysis*
- "Market perpetually fluctuates; astute investors leverage this volatility rather than fear it." · Benjamin Graham · *The Intelligent Investor*

## Growth & Quality

- "不要拔掉鲜花浇灌野草" — Don't abandon quality holdings to chase inferior ones. · Peter Lynch · *One Up on Wall Street*
- The best place to find 10-baggers is in your daily life; invest in what you know before Wall Street discovers it. · Peter Lynch · *One Up on Wall Street*
- Conduct on-site investigation before investing; never rely solely on broker research. · Peter Lynch · *One Up on Wall Street*
- Apply differentiated analysis across company types — different factors matter for different kinds of businesses. · Peter Lynch · *Beating the Street*

## Passive vs. Active

- "Stock price short-term fluctuations cannot be predicted; neither fundamental nor technical analysis can consistently beat the market." · Burton G. Malkiel · *A Random Walk Down Wall Street*
- Passive index fund investing is the most reliable strategy for most investors; minimizing transaction frequency reduces both tax burden and behavioral error. · Burton G. Malkiel · *A Random Walk Down Wall Street*
- "Investors are emotional beings; stock psychology significantly impacts valuations." · Burton G. Malkiel · *A Random Walk Down Wall Street*

## Quantitative & Systematic

- "Buy cheap good companies" — combining value assessment with quality evaluation through quantitative metrics beats subjective judgment. · Joel Greenblatt · *The Little Book That Beats the Market*
- Long-term perspective is essential — the Magic Formula often underperforms for consecutive years before outperforming; requires 3–5+ year commitment. · Joel Greenblatt · *The Little Book That Beats the Market*
- Passive, mechanical investment systems often outperform human-managed systems, even professional fund managers. · James O'Shaughnessy · *What Works on Wall Street* (4th Edition)
- Employ empirically-validated strategies combining multiple factors rather than single-factor approaches. · James O'Shaughnessy · *What Works on Wall Street* (4th Edition)

## Concentration & Deep Research

- "Concentrate your portfolio to maximize conviction" — holding typically 15 or fewer positions enables deeper analysis and higher conviction bets. · Michael Lauer · *Stock Market Wizards* (Jack Schwager)
- "Understand completely before investing" — only invest in companies and industries you genuinely comprehend; leverage executive networks to build knowledge. · Michael Lauer · *Stock Market Wizards* (Jack Schwager)
- "Find opportunity in distress" — seek stocks experiencing major declines (50%+ below market average) as these often contain temporary setbacks hiding real value. · Michael Lauer · *Stock Market Wizards* (Jack Schwager)
- "Balance sheets trump income statements" — prioritize debt sustainability relative to cash flow over short-term profitability metrics. · Michael Lauer · *Stock Market Wizards* (Jack Schwager)
- "Investment is very meticulous work" — concentrated positions in high-quality companies with average 20-stock portfolio and 6+ year holding periods. · David Polen · Polen Capital Management

## Small-Cap & Overlooked

- "Many companies are never seen by the masses" — opportunity lies in overlooked securities across natural resources, biotech, and tech sectors. · Michael A. Berry · *Financial Analysts Journal* (with David Dreman)
- Value investing requires balancing cheapness with growth potential, not pursuing either extreme alone. · Michael A. Berry & David N. Dreman · "Overreaction, Underreaction, and the Low-P/E Effect," *Financial Analysts Journal*
- Using forward-looking (predicted) metrics reduces risks from earnings volatility better than historical data. · Michael A. Berry · *Financial Analysts Journal*
- "I like situations where an unpopular industry leader turns adversity into advantage." · W. Whitney George · Royce Funds / Wall Street interviews
- Strong balance sheets and substantial cash reserves are essential for stability in small-cap investing. · W. Whitney George · Royce Funds

## Holding Period Discipline

- "If you are uncomfortable holding a stock for 10 years, you shouldn't hold it for 10 minutes." · Warren Buffett · Berkshire Hathaway Shareholder Letters
- "The stock market is a device for transferring money from the impatient to the patient." · Warren Buffett · Berkshire Hathaway Shareholder Letters

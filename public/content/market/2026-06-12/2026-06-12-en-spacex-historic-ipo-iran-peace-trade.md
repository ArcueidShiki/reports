## Market Narrative

Friday, June 12, 2026 will be remembered as the day Wall Street priced two regime changes at once: the largest initial public offering in history and the apparent end of the US–Iran war. SpaceX (SPCX) sold 555.6 million shares at a fixed $135 offering price, raising roughly $75 billion at a $1.77 trillion starting valuation, and the stock never looked back — opening at $150 at 11:48 a.m. ET after an extended price-discovery process, tagging $176 at its high, and settling at $161.11, up 19.34%, for an end-of-day market value around $2.1 trillion. The debut instantly made Elon Musk the world's first trillionaire, adding an estimated $188 billion to his net worth and lifting his fortune to about $1.1 trillion. The fact that the index machinery moved in real time was just as consequential: MSCI added SPCX to its Standard and Large-Cap indexes on day one, and Nasdaq's new fast-track rules opened the door to passive inclusion almost immediately.

The second engine of the rally was geopolitical. President Trump said he canceled planned overnight strikes on Iran because a "very strong memorandum of understanding" had been reached. By Friday morning, Iranian state media had described a 14-point draft under which Washington would lift oil sanctions, release half of Iran's frozen funds and end the naval blockade, while Tehran would reopen the Strait of Hormuz within 30 days; Bloomberg reported a final agreement could be signed in Switzerland as soon as Sunday, timed around the June 15–17 G7 summit in Evian. Crude responded immediately: WTI fell roughly 4% to settle below $85 — an eight-week low — and Brent dropped below $86.50, its lowest since early March. After a week in which the Dow swung from a 900-point loss (Wednesday, on threats of more strikes) to a 929-point gain (Thursday, on the cancellation), Friday's session was an orderly continuation rather than a melt-up.

Under the surface, the day's character was rotation, not euphoria. Nine of eleven S&P sectors advanced, led by materials, financials and utilities, and the equal-weight S&P rose almost 2% — four times the cap-weighted index — while mega-cap growth fell about 1% as a group, with Amazon down 2.17% and Apple off 1.95% late in the session. Roughly 62% of US equities were advancing at midday, and small caps led the tape from the opening bell. That breadth profile suggests the SpaceX debut did not drain liquidity from the broader market — the classic fear with giant IPOs — but instead coincided with money rotating out of crowded mega-cap tech into everything else, helped by the prospect of cheaper oil and a de-escalating Middle East.

The tension investors must hold in their heads heading into next week: the peace trade is colliding with an inflation problem the Fed has not solved. May CPI printed a three-year high of 4.2% on Wednesday and May PPI ran hotter than forecast at 6.5% year over year on Thursday, almost entirely on war-driven energy costs. The 10-year Treasury yield edged up 1.8 basis points to 4.483% on Friday, the 20- and 30-year sit just below 5%, and the 2-year near 4.15% is trading well above the 3.50%–3.75% funds rate — the bond market is literally pricing a hike, not a cut, as new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh walks into his first FOMC meeting on June 16–17 with the White House publicly demanding lower rates. Peace would resolve the supply shock; it does not resolve the institutional collision.

## Macro & Economic Data

**May CPI (released Wed 6/10):** headline +0.5% MoM, +4.2% YoY — the hottest annual rate in more than three years, in line with consensus. Core CPI rose just +0.2% MoM, cooler than expected and a downshift from April's +0.4%. The composition is the message: energy rose 3.9% on the month and 23.5% YoY, gasoline is up 40.5% YoY, and energy alone contributed over 60% of the monthly increase. This is a supply-shock CPI, not a demand-overheat CPI — which is precisely why a Hormuz reopening matters more to the inflation path right now than anything the Fed can do.

**May PPI (released Thu 6/11):** +1.1% MoM versus +0.7% expected; +6.5% YoY, the highest wholesale inflation since November 2022. Core PPI rose 0.4% (a tenth below consensus). Final-demand goods prices surged 2.8% — the largest increase in the series' history back to December 2009 — with energy up 10.7% and wholesale gasoline up 23.4%. Stage-1 intermediate demand jumped 3.2% MoM (a series record) and 12.3% YoY, the worst pipeline pressure since June 2022. Pipeline pressure this severe normally bleeds into core CPI with a one-to-two-quarter lag — unless the energy input reverses first, which is exactly what the oil market began pricing on Friday.

**UMich Consumer Sentiment, June preliminary (released Fri 6/12):** 48.9 versus 46 expected, up about 9% from May's record low of 44.8 — the first improvement in four months, attributed to easing gas prices, with lower-income cohorts improving most. One-year inflation expectations eased to 4.6% (from 4.8%) and long-run expectations fell to 3.4% (from 3.9%). Still bleak in level terms: sentiment remains 13% below January and roughly 19% below a year ago. For the Fed, the drop in long-run expectations is the single most important number in the release — it argues expectations remain anchored despite a 4-handle CPI.

**Rates & Fed pricing:** 10Y 4.483% (+1.8 bps); 20Y/30Y just under 5%; 2Y ≈4.15%, implying the market prices at least one 25 bp hike by end-2026 despite a funds rate of 3.50%–3.75% held steady at the January, March and April meetings. CME FedWatch puts >98% odds on a hold next week; Polymarket odds of a 2026 hike spiked to 62% over the prior weekend before settling near 54%. The 2s10s curve holds a modest positive slope of roughly +33 bps.

## Fed, Rates & Credit

The defining macro fact of the week is that the bond market and the White House want opposite things from the same brand-new Fed chair. Kevin Warsh, sworn in May 22 after Jerome Powell's term ended, chairs his first FOMC meeting Tuesday–Wednesday — a Summary of Economic Projections meeting with a fresh dot plot. Markets assign >98% odds to a hold at 3.50%–3.75%, so the entire event is about language: most analysts expect the statement's easing bias to be replaced with something neutral-to-hawkish, and the dot plot will reveal whether the committee's center of gravity has shifted toward the hike the 2-year is already pricing. Ed Yardeni argues the Fed may be forced to hike as soon as July to "appease bond vigilantes." Meanwhile the administration spent Friday publicly pressing for "drastically lower" rates — Trump has said a rate increase "would be wrong" — setting up genuine institutional-credibility risk if Warsh's Fed even hints at tightening. Watch the dissent pattern and the long end: 30-year yields knocking on 5% with gold at $4,200+ is the market hedging a Fed-independence premium, not just an inflation premium.

## Geopolitical & Policy

**US–Iran:** event → transmission → impact. The cancellation of strikes and the 14-point draft MOU (sanctions relief + frozen-fund release + blockade end, in exchange for Hormuz reopening within 30 days and verified de-nuclearization, plus a reported $300 billion reconstruction framework) directly attacks the supply-side channel that produced a 4.2% CPI: reopened Hormuz → tanker flow normalizes → crude and refined-product risk premium deflates (WTI already −4% to an 8-week low) → gasoline's 40.5% YoY contribution to CPI mechanically unwinds over the summer → breakevens and hike odds fall → relief for duration and for energy-sensitive cyclicals; offsetting losers are energy producers and defense names. The deal could be signed Sunday in Switzerland around the G7. The risk case: drafts have died before — April's ceasefire broke down into renewed strikes by late May — and a failure at the signing table would put the entire week's rally and the oil move back in play, in reverse.

**Paramount–WBD:** after the close, the DOJ's Antitrust Division approved Paramount Skydance's roughly $110–111 billion acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery with no divestitures or behavioral remedies. WBD added ~0.6% after hours; PSKY ticked up to $10.62 (+$0.14). Close is targeted for September; state attorneys general may still sue, and the EU review runs to a July 14 deadline. Transmission: a consolidation green light this permissive resets M&A expectations across media and beyond — expect bankers to shop everything.

**Tariff policy:** Walmart said it would lower prices following tariff refunds — a reminder that the 2025 tariff regime is partially unwinding through the courts and refund process, a disinflationary undercurrent nobody is pricing while energy dominates the conversation.

## Focus Deep Dive — SpaceX (SPCX): The $2 Trillion Debut

### Price Action & Technicals
- **Day-one map (the only levels that exist):** offering price $135 = structural floor and the level underwriters will defend; opening print $150 = first support; close $161.11; session high $176 = resistance. The stock faded from $176 in the final half hour on profit-taking — a normal day-one pattern, but it defines the near-term range: $150–$176.
- **Bollinger Bands / MACD:** not yet computable on one session; the indicators worth watching instead are the day-one VWAP zone (roughly mid-$150s to low-$160s given where volume concentrated after the open) and whether day two holds above the $150 opening print. First close below $150 would signal allocation flippers overwhelming index demand.
- **Volume:** the debut "smashed trading volumes" per TheStreet — record IPO turnover. Heavy retail allocation (the deal was deliberately retail-tilted and heavily oversubscribed) means flow is less sticky than a typical institutional book.
- **Options positioning:** listed options were not yet trading on debut day; expect listing within days. Until then there is no max-pain or gamma map — which itself matters, because dealers cannot recycle risk and spot moves are unhedged. When options list, the first visible structure will likely cluster at $150/$160/$175 strikes mirroring the day-one range.
- **Index flows are the real technical:** MSCI added SPCX to Standard and Large-Cap indexes on day one; Nasdaq's new rules permit rapid index entry. Forced passive buying over the coming days-to-weeks is measurable, price-insensitive demand — the strongest argument the $135–$150 zone holds.

### Fundamental Context
SpaceX raised ~$75 billion — the largest IPO ever — at a $1.77 trillion offer valuation, closing near $2.1 trillion. Starlink is the primary revenue driver; the February acquisition of xAI folded Grok and the AI story into the listing, making SPCX simultaneously a space-infrastructure, telecom and AI bet. The risk ledger: (1) the post-IPO lockup expiry later this year will release enormous supply ("a lot of stock to digest," as Rev Shark DePorre put it); (2) key-man concentration on Musk, now managing a $1.1 trillion fortune across two public companies; (3) Grok-related regulatory and reputational exposure, already the subject of protests at the listing; (4) valuation — at ~$2.1 trillion, SPCX is priced above almost every company on earth on day one, on a fraction of their cash flow. For the sector, day one confirmed the funding-vacuum thesis: SPCX up 19% while ASTS, RKLB, LUNR and SPCE all bled.

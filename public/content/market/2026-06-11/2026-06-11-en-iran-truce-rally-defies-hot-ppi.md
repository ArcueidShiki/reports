## Market Narrative

For the second time this week, the entire market day was written by a single man's social media account — and this time the post was bullish. After Wednesday night's fresh round of US airstrikes on Iran and Tehran's declaration that the Strait of Hormuz was "completely closed to all vessels," Thursday opened with futures modestly green on hopes the swift conclusion of the strikes signaled an off-ramp. Those hopes were nearly derailed at 8:30 a.m. by the hottest wholesale inflation print in three and a half years: May PPI rose 1.1% on the month against a 0.7% consensus, with the annual rate hitting 6.5% — the highest since November 2022. Stocks wobbled, held early gains, and then the afternoon detonated higher when President Trump posted that he had cancelled the strikes scheduled for Thursday evening, that negotiations "have been brought to the highest level of Iranian leadership and approved," and that a time and place for a signing would "be announced shortly."

The repricing was violent and broad. The Dow surged 929.97 points (+1.86%) to 50,848.75, reclaiming the 50,000 level it lost only a day earlier. The S&P 500 rose 1.75% to close just shy of 7,400, the Nasdaq Composite jumped 2.54%, and the Russell 2000 — the index most leveraged to domestic financial conditions and energy costs — led everything at +3.02%. The VIX collapsed roughly 12% back below 20, unwinding Wednesday's entire fear spike in a single session. Crude oil, the transmission belt connecting this war to every inflation print, fell more than 4% toward $86 WTI — its lowest level since April — after losing as much as 11% in the immediate aftermath of Trump's post.

Beneath the euphoria, the day's internals tell a more conditional story. Sector leadership was tech, industrials and materials, while energy, staples and real estate — the three places investors had been hiding — declined. That is a textbook unwind of war-hedge positioning, not a fresh-money bull thrust. And the bond market did not celebrate: with PPI at 6.5% YoY stacked on Wednesday's 4.2% CPI, the rates market still has to price next week's June 16–17 FOMC — Kevin Warsh's first meeting as Chair — where a hold is near-certain (~98% per CME FedWatch) but the dot plot could formalize the hike bias that futures have been flirting with all month (~70% odds of a hike by year-end as of Wednesday).

The third storyline running under everything: SpaceX. The offering book closed Thursday evening at a fixed $135 per share, valuing the company at $1.77 trillion and raising $75 billion — the largest IPO in history by a factor of 2.5x over Saudi Aramco's $29.4 billion. SPCX debuts on the Nasdaq Friday. As Jim Cramer put it, this single IPO "will determine market action for the next month": it is simultaneously a colossal liquidity drain, a sentiment referendum on the AI/space trade, and — at the seventh-largest US market cap, above Tesla — an instant index-relevant mega-cap.

## Macro & Economic Data

**May PPI (released 8:30 a.m. ET): the inflation pipeline is getting worse, not better.**
- Headline PPI: **+1.1% MoM** vs +0.7% consensus (prior: +1.1%) — second consecutive 1%+ monthly print
- Core PPI: **+0.4% MoM**, in line with consensus
- Headline YoY: **+6.5%** vs +6.4% consensus — largest 12-month rise since November 2022 (+7.4%)

The composition is the story. Nearly 80% of the May advance came from final-demand goods (+2.8%), of which over half traces to a 23.4% monthly surge in gasoline; final-demand energy jumped 10.7%. This is the Hormuz closure showing up in producer costs with a lag, layered on top of the tariff-driven goods inflation that has been creeping up for a year. PPI leads CPI by one to three months in the goods channel — meaning Wednesday's 4.2% CPI is not the peak unless crude's truce-driven break below $90 holds. The implication for the Fed is uncomfortable: input-cost inflation at 6.5% with headline CPI at 4.2% and a funds rate of 3.50%–3.75% leaves the real policy rate negative, a configuration that historically ends with hikes, not cuts.

**ECB hikes.** The European Central Bank raised its deposit rate 25bp to 2.25% — its first hike since 2023 — confirming that the global central-bank cycle has turned in response to the energy shock. When the ECB validates the inflation regime, it is harder for US rates markets to dismiss the Fed's hawkish tail.

**Rates & Fed pricing.** The 10-year Treasury entered the day near 4.54% after Wednesday's CPI-driven selloff; the long end stayed heavy even as equities rallied — the bond market is trading the inflation data, not the truce headlines. CME FedWatch prices a ~98% hold for June 16–17 (Chair Warsh's first meeting), with roughly 70% odds of a *hike* by year-end as of midweek. The dot plot, not the rate decision, is next week's true event.

## Market Narrative

Friday, June 5 delivered the week's most anticipated macro catalyst — the May nonfarm payrolls report — and it landed firmly on the hawkish side of expectations. The economy added more jobs than the consensus called for (economists had estimated a range of 80,000 to 105,000), with private payrolls echoing the prior day's ADP beat of 122,000. The unemployment rate held steady at 4.3%, and average hourly earnings continued to signal a labor market that, despite clear signs of deceleration, refuses to fully roll over. The headline that greeted traders at 8:30 a.m. ET: a "strong jobs report" — and the immediate interpretation was that the Federal Reserve has even less room to maneuver on rate cuts in 2026.

The reaction across asset classes was coherent and telling. Equities ended the day "little changed" — the S&P 500 barely moved from Thursday's close — but the composition of that flatness revealed the market's internals. Treasury yields rose, gold sold off sharply (-1.19%), small-caps underperformed as higher-for-longer hurt rate-sensitive names, and the VIX edged up 3.55% to 16.33, reflecting genuine options activity around the event. Meanwhile, AI-adjacent mega-cap tech found no relief: NVIDIA fell 3.14% to $215.82, extending the chip sector's rough week following Broadcom's post-earnings tumble on Thursday. Notably, the S&P 500 still secured its 10th consecutive positive weekly close — the longest streak since 1985 — with the index up a fractional amount on the week, preserving an extraordinary bull run that has tacked on roughly 16% since January 1, 2026.

The week as a whole was dominated by a shifting of the AI chip narrative. After months in which semiconductor stocks could do no wrong, Broadcom's (AVGO) earnings on Wednesday evening (June 3) cracked the veneer. AVGO plunged roughly 15% on Thursday after investors decided that even record AI semiconductor revenue and strong guidance were insufficient to justify the stock's elevated valuation. CrowdStrike (CRWD) fell approximately 9% on similar "sell the news" dynamics. That created a broad rotation on Thursday: the Dow Jones surged 1.73% as investors piled into non-tech names, while the Nasdaq barely broke even. By Friday, a strong jobs print ensured that even the rotation trade had limited room to run — higher yields pressure all equities at the margin.

## Sector & Theme Breakdown

**AI / Mega-Cap Tech.** The "Magnificent Seven" divergence continued. NVIDIA closed Friday at $215.82 (-3.14%), dragged down by the broader chip pullback and fears that the bar for AI-related beats keeps rising. Earlier in the week, NVDA, Microsoft, and Apple had traded near fresh all-time highs, while Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, and Tesla were each off their spring peaks. The fraying of the AI monolith is a theme that Schwab's market strategists flagged explicitly: "There's more of a fraying of names, and stocks aren't moving as a monolith." Signs of speculative froth — rallies in non-profitable tech and heavily shorted names — are adding caution to the near-term picture.

**AI Chips / Semiconductors.** The PHLX Semiconductor Index (SOX) surged 22.1% between April and late May — the greatest month-long run in years — setting up a high-expectation environment for earnings. Broadcom's disappointment illustrated that even a 200%+ AI revenue growth forecast can disappoint if guidance falls short of investor hopes. Micron (MU) and Super Micro Computer (SMCI) each fell 5-7% on Thursday in sympathy. Intel (INTC) also declined. By contrast, Marvell Technology (MRVL) had surged 33% earlier in the week after Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang described it as a potential next trillion-dollar company at Computex 2026.

**Space.** SpaceX stole the week's headlines by pricing its IPO at $135 per share, implying a $1.77 trillion valuation and a planned Nasdaq debut on June 12. At that price, SpaceX overtakes Tesla ($1.6T) to become the seventh-largest U.S. company by market cap. Goldman Sachs led the underwriting, with Morgan Stanley, BofA, Citi, and JPMorgan as joint bookrunners. Elon Musk retains 82%+ voting control. The offering will raise approximately $75 billion, making it one of the largest IPOs in history.

**Optical Communications.** Ciena (CIEN) was a case study in "sell the news": the company reported earnings that beat both EPS and revenue estimates, with revenue climbing 40% year-over-year, and even raised guidance — yet the stock plunged 8% on Thursday. The optical networking sector had already priced in significant AI data center-driven demand, and the reaction suggests institutional profit-taking at a moment of peak enthusiasm.

**Software / Cloud.** The theme rotation toward software on chip-weakness days persisted. ServiceNow (NOW) and Palantir (PLTR) each advanced roughly 3% on Thursday when chips fell. Software has increasingly been viewed as the "other leg" of the AI trade — less exposed to near-term capex disappointment and more tied to enterprise contract revenue.

**Energy / Power.** Oil eased on Friday, with WTI crude at approximately $92.40 (down from $95.17 Thursday morning), as diplomatic signals from the Iran-US negotiations offered marginal relief. WTI remains historically elevated — above $90 since the Strait of Hormuz was effectively closed more than 100 days ago. About 15 million barrels of oil per day remain offline. S&P Global estimates it will take up to six months to return to 80% of pre-crisis supply even after full resolution, and damaged Persian Gulf infrastructure could take years to fully repair.

- **Secondary support:** $200–$202 — the 50-day MA region and a key psychological level. A close below $210 opens the door to this zone.

### Bollinger Bands Status
NVDA's three-day pullback from the upper Bollinger Band (near $228–$230) toward the middle band (approximately $210–$212) is a textbook mean-reversion pattern following a parabolic move. The bandwidth has been expanding (high volatility environment), meaning the middle band is not a strong floor on its own. Watch for a close back above the middle band (~$213–$215 area) as confirmation that the pullback is constructive rather than trend-reversing.

### MACD Signal
The MACD on the daily chart is showing an early-stage bearish cross, with the MACD line crossing below the signal line around the $220 area. Histogram bars have turned negative. This is a caution signal, though in a structurally bullish market it often resolves as a brief consolidation rather than a sustained reversal. The prior three MACD bearish crosses in NVDA's 2025–2026 bull run were all buyable pullbacks of 8–15%.

### Volume & Order Flow (Institutional Signals)
Thursday's chip selloff was accompanied by 2–3x average volume in NVDA, suggesting institutional repositioning rather than retail-driven selling. The pattern of large-cap tech stocks absorbing institutional selling while small-caps underperform is consistent with portfolio managers reducing concentration risk post-a major earnings event (AVGO acting as a proxy for the entire AI chip basket). Notably, software stocks (NOW, PLTR) popped 3% on the same day — a classic rotation footprint of institutional money rotating within the tech complex rather than leaving it.

### Options Wall / Max Pain Level
With NVDA near $215, the options max pain level for the nearest expiry (mid-June) is estimated in the $210–$215 range based on open interest concentration. Market makers have incentive to pin the stock near this level into June expiry. A break decisively above $220 into next week would be bullish; a drift below $210 toward the $205–$207 range would signal more serious selling pressure and potentially test the 50-day MA.

### Fundamental Context
NVIDIA remains the central artery of the AI trade. Its H100 and Blackwell GPU architectures are in backorder across hyperscalers, and data center revenue for the most recent quarter was the largest in company history. The key fundamental question is whether AI capex by Microsoft, Amazon, Google, and Meta remains on track — and there have been no signals of a slowdown in hyperscaler spending. Jensen Huang's endorsement of Marvell as a potential trillion-dollar company at Computex underscores the confidence in AI semiconductor demand breadth extending beyond NVDA itself. The risk is valuation: NVDA trades at a significant premium to historical tech multiples, and any signal of demand deceleration — even a single quarter of guidance miss — could trigger a sharper correction.

**The Broader Lesson of This Week:** Broadcom's result (strong beat, raised guidance, still -15%) is a warning sign about how high the bar has been set for AI chip names. Investors should be aware that even robust fundamental performance can be punished if the whisper number is even stronger. For NVDA's upcoming earnings (expected late August), the bar will again be extraordinary.

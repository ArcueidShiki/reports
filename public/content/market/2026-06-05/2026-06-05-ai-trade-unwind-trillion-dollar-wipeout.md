## Market Narrative

Friday, June 5 was the day the AI melt-up finally met a wall — and the wall had two bricks in it. The first was Broadcom. The second was a labor market that simply refused to cooperate with the rate-cut narrative the equity market had spent the spring pricing in. Together they produced the worst session for the Nasdaq Composite since April 2025, a roughly $1 trillion mark-down in AI-related equity value, and a violent repricing of Federal Reserve expectations from "cuts coming" to "the next move might be a hike."

The setup mattered. Just three sessions earlier, on June 2, the S&P 500 closed above 7,600 for the first time ever, the culmination of a chip-led boom that had carried the index to a string of records. The Dow had printed a fresh record as recently as Thursday. In other words, the market entered Friday priced for perfection, with the heaviest weighting in history concentrated in a handful of AI semiconductor and mega-cap names. When perfection didn't arrive, there was a long way to fall.

The trigger was Broadcom's fiscal Q2 report, delivered after the close on June 3. On almost any absolute measure the print was excellent — revenue up 48% year over year, AI semiconductor revenue up 143% — yet the stock cratered as much as 13% over the following two sessions because management left its full-year AI chip targets effectively unchanged and flagged a slower custom-accelerator (XPU) ramp at two large hyperscale customers. For a stock priced for ever-accelerating guidance raises, "merely excellent and unchanged" was a sell signal. The disappointment metastasized: Nvidia fell 5.93%, Micron dropped roughly 13%, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index extended a two-day decline of about 10.4%, with Arm and Intel also among the hardest hit.

Then, at 8:30 a.m. ET, the May employment report poured accelerant on the fire. Nonfarm payrolls rose 172,000 versus a Dow Jones consensus of just 80,000 — more than double expectations and above the entire forecast range. A strong labor market is normally good news, but in a market leaning on imminent rate relief, it was read as the opposite: yields spiked, the 2-year jumped to its highest since February 2025, and CME FedWatch swung to pricing roughly a 70% chance the Fed's next move is a *hike* by year-end, with essentially no cut priced. Rate-sensitive growth names and small caps took the brunt, and the VIX exploded 34% higher.

- **VIX / options:** A 34% VIX spike above 20 marks a regime change in implied volatility. Dealers who were long gamma into the record highs flipped to short gamma as spot collapsed, mechanically amplifying the move — sell-offs beget more selling as hedgers chase. Max-pain and put walls below current levels become magnets into next week's expiry.
- **Volume & order flow:** The breadth of the decline (semis, mega-cap, small caps all down hard) plus the volume signature points to broad institutional de-grossing rather than a narrow, single-name event.

### Fundamental Context

The fundamental question is whether this is a valuation reset inside an intact secular AI buildout, or the first crack in the thesis itself. The bull case is that Broadcom's actual numbers — 143% AI growth, a path to $56B this year and >$100B in FY27 — confirm demand is real and enormous; the sell-off is simply expectations catching down to a still-spectacular reality. The bear case is that the "slower XPU ramp at two hyperscalers" is the canary: if even the best-positioned custom-silicon vendor is seeing digestion at its largest customers, the market's straight-line extrapolation of AI capex was too aggressive. The truth is likely that the *demand* is intact but the *multiple* was not — when names are priced for guidance raises every quarter, the math only works until one quarter it doesn't. That quarter was this one.

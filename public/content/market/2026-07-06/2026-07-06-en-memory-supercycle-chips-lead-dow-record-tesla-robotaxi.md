## Market Narrative

Wall Street came back from the long Independence Day weekend in a buying mood, and for the first time in over a week the buying was led by exactly the group that had been the market's problem child: semiconductors. The Nasdaq Composite jumped 1.12% to 26,121.16, the S&P 500 added 0.72% to 7,537.43, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average squeezed out another record close at 53,055.91. On the surface it looked like a clean, broad, risk-on Monday. Underneath, it was a very specific bet — that the artificial-intelligence hardware cycle, far from topping out, is entering its most powerful and most *profitable* phase yet, this time routed through memory and storage rather than GPUs.

The tell was in the leadership. The VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH) surged roughly 3.6%, its best session in weeks, and the move was powered not by Nvidia but by the memory and storage complex. Western Digital rocketed about 7.7% on a wave of analyst price-target increases, Teradyne climbed 2.8%, and Broadcom rose 3.7% on a fresh multi-year Apple contract. The catalyst underneath all of it: a synchronized surge in DRAM and NAND pricing that is turning memory from a low-margin commodity drag into a genuine profit engine, just as Samsung prepares to report a record quarter and SK Hynix lines up a $28 billion U.S. listing. After two sessions (June 30 and July 1–2) where the chip trade was alternately the only leg holding the market up and then the leg dragging it down, July 6 was the day the AI-hardware bulls reclaimed the narrative — and the money followed.

But the flat-looking index masked a two-speed tape. The mega-cap growth names roared — Tesla +7.35%, Meta +4.90%, Apple +4.88% — while the Russell 2000 small-cap index actually *fell* 0.55% and energy stocks were routed as crude oil slid to a four-month low. This was not a "risk-on-everything" rally; it was a concentrated bid for the AI winners and a simultaneous unwind of the geopolitical "war premium" and the defensive corners of the market. Breadth, in other words, was narrower than the record headlines suggested. The people cheering the Dow's all-time high and the people who owned small caps or oil majors had very different Mondays.

Framing all of it was a subtle but important shift in the rates picture. Ever since new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's pointedly hawkish debut on June 17 — no forward guidance, no dot plot, and a vow to "fix" inflation — the market had been bracing for the possibility of a rate *hike*, not a cut. But Thursday's soft June jobs report (just +57,000 payrolls) has begun to erode that hike bias, and by Monday the futures-implied odds of a September hike had slipped back toward 50% from around 64% a day earlier. With FOMC minutes due Wednesday, traders leaned into the idea that the most hawkish scenario is now less likely — and a market that had feared higher rates found room to rally.

## Sector & Theme Breakdown

**Memory & AI Storage — the day's engine.** The single most important theme was the memory supercycle. Western Digital (WDC) closed near $592.71, up roughly 7.7%, after Cantor Fitzgerald lifted its target from $660 to $900 and Melius Research initiated at Buy with a striking $1,050 objective, both tying the calls to a multi-year, supply-constrained AI storage cycle. The industry math is stark: analysts see demand for AI-data-center hard drives growing 40–50% annually against supply growth of only 30–35%. The read-through rippled across the complex — Teradyne +2.8%, Micron and the broader memory names firming — ahead of Samsung's Q2 report (expected July 7) and SK Hynix's planned Nasdaq ADR debut around July 10. DRAM prices reportedly rose 44% quarter-on-quarter and NAND flash climbed 53%, a synchronized spike that reprices the entire group's earnings power.

**Mega-cap tech — narrative repricings.** Tesla (TSLA) led the Magnificent Seven with a 7.35% surge to about $419.77 (more below). Meta (META) rose 4.90% as its "Meta Compute" cloud initiative — leasing idle AI datacenter capacity to outside clients — continued to reframe its enormous capex as a potential revenue line rather than a pure cost. Apple (AAPL) gained 4.88% after Broadcom extended its custom-silicon partnership with Apple through 2031, removing a long-standing overhang about Apple designing Broadcom out. Microsoft added 1.69%, Nvidia 1.39%, Amazon 0.55%, and Alphabet a muted 0.23%.

**Semiconductors ex-memory.** Broadcom (AVGO) rose 3.7% on the Apple deal — significant because Apple is estimated to account for roughly 20% of Broadcom's annual revenue, and the extension eases fears of near-term share loss. The rest of the complex (AMD, Micron, AMAT, LRCX, KLAC, ASML, TSM) participated in the broad SMH lift on the memory-led, risk-on tone.

**Space — profit-taking after a moonshot.** After a euphoric prior week (Rocket Lab +25%, AST SpaceMobile +30%), the space names pulled back hard: Rocket Lab (RKLB) fell about 7%, AST SpaceMobile (ASTS) dropped 5%, and Intuitive Machines (LUNR) slipped roughly 3%. Retail enthusiasm cooled visibly — a widely-shared "RKLB 2900→29k" social-media victory-lap post on July 2 read, in hindsight, like a classic local-top exit signal. Rocket Lab's longer-term story (a landmark U.S. Space Force launch and an $8 billion Iridium acquisition) remains intact; this was positioning, not thesis, unwinding.

**AI neoclouds — collateral damage.** The flip side of Meta's monetization pivot: CoreWeave (CRWV) had cratered about 13.9% to $85.68 and Nebius (NBIS) sank 17% to $229.18 in the prior sessions on fears that Meta Compute will undercut GPU-rental pricing. Meta is a major customer of both (a disclosed $21 billion commitment from CoreWeave, up to $27 billion from Nebius), so the threat cuts especially deep. The neocloud complex remained the market's clearest AI-monetization casualty.

**Energy & power — the war premium bleeds out.** Oil majors were the day's worst mega-caps: ExxonMobil (XOM) fell 2.82% to $139.73 and Chevron (CVX) dropped 2.81% to $175.98, both now roughly 20–23% below their 52-week highs. The driver was policy, not fundamentals: OFAC's 60-day Iran oil general license is flooding the market with crude and deflating the geopolitical premium that had carried the majors to their 2026 peaks. The AI-power theme (Constellation, Vistra) stayed structurally supported by hyperscaler nuclear deals but was not the day's focus.

**Optical communications — still nursing last week's reset.** The optical group (Ciena, Coherent, Lumentum) was the prior week's casualty, with CIEN −8.65% and COHR/LITE each down roughly 10% on July 2 in a valuation-driven, sector-wide reset triggered by a research report questioning the pace of co-packaged-optics adoption. The group stabilized into July 6 but did not lead.

## Macro & Economic Data

**June Employment Situation (released July 2, still driving the tape):** Nonfarm payrolls rose just **+57,000**, badly missing the ~+115,000 consensus, with May revised *down* to +129,000. The unemployment rate actually *fell* to **4.2%** — but for the wrong reason: labor-force participation dropped 0.3 percentage point to **61.5%**, the lowest since March 2021. Professional & business services (+36k), social assistance (+25k), and healthcare (+22k) carried the gains, while leisure & hospitality *lost* 61,000 jobs on weak seasonal hiring. **Interpretation:** this is a "decompose-the-good-number" print. A falling unemployment rate built on people *leaving* the workforce is a slowing-labor signal dressed up as strength, not a sign of a tight labor market. Crucially, in the current regime the soft data does not force a *cut* — Warsh is anchored on inflation — but it can push a *hike* further out, which is exactly how the market read it.

**ISM Services PMI, June (released July 6):** Headline **54.0**, down 0.5 point from May's 54.5 but still the 24th consecutive month of expansion. Business Activity fell to 55.4 (from 57.7), New Orders eased to 55.1 (from 57.3), but the Employment index jumped to **51.2** from 47.9 — its first expansion in four months. **Interpretation:** the services economy is still growing, just more slowly, and the employment sub-index quietly contradicts the soft payrolls print — a reminder that the labor picture is decelerating, not collapsing. This is a "slowing, not stalling" data point, supportive of the soft-landing-with-sticky-inflation base case.

**FOMC minutes (June 17 meeting) — due Wednesday, July 8:** The market's next macro catalyst. Traders want detail on how hawkish the Warsh Fed's internal debate really was, and whether the "almost half of members projecting at least one 2026 hike" dot-plot signal reflects broad conviction or a hawkish tail.

Together they are the fundamental spine of Monday's memory rally.

## Fed, Rates & Credit

The federal funds target sits at **3.50%–3.75%**, held on June 17 at Chair Warsh's first meeting. The 10-year Treasury yield hovered around **4.47%**. The front end is where the action is: the 2-year, which had been pinned above the top of the funds range on the hike-bias, has been easing as the soft June jobs report trims the odds of near-term tightening — the 2s10s curve remains modestly positive but is being reshaped from the front. September hike odds slipped toward **~50%** from ~64% a day earlier.

Warsh himself has softened his tone from the June debut. Speaking around the ECB's Sintra forum, he noted inflation expectations had eased over the past month and signaled no urgency to raise rates, while on July 1 he declined to commit to a July hike, emphasizing a "data-dependent" approach and anticipating a "lively discussion" among policymakers. **Net:** the regime is still hike-vs-hold, not cut-vs-hold — but Monday's rally reflects a market pricing the *hold* leg with more confidence than a week ago. The July 29 FOMC remains the real test of whether Warsh actually pulls the trigger.

## Geopolitical & Policy

The dominant policy transmission ran through oil. OFAC's issuance of a **60-day Iran oil general license** is allowing a wave of Iranian crude back into global markets. The chain is clean: more supply → WTI slides below **$69** (its lowest since late February) → the geopolitical "war premium" that had lifted XOM and CVX to 2026 highs deflates → energy equities fall ~2.8%. The second-order effect is disinflationary and, on balance, *supportive* of risk assets and of the Fed's inflation fight — cheaper energy eases headline CPI and buys the Warsh Fed room to hold rather than hike. The subtlety flagged in prior notes still applies: soft oil is a disinflation tailwind, but persistently weak crude can also signal soft *global demand*, which rhymes with the cooling U.S. labor data. Cheap energy is a gift to consumers and a warning to growth bulls at the same time.

## Focus Deep Dive — Tesla (TSLA): The Robotaxi Re-Rating

Tesla was the single most dramatic stock story of the day, surging **7.35% to about $419.77** — a near-complete reversal of its ~7% plunge on July 2. What changed was not the fundamentals but the *narrative*, and the sequencing is instructive. On July 2 the market *sold* a headline Q2 delivery beat (480,126 vehicles delivered vs ~397k expected) because it decomposed the number: Tesla *produced* only 451,758 units and *delivered* 480,126, meaning the quarter drained inventory rather than reflecting fresh organic demand, and deliveries say nothing about the margin that lands July 22. Four sessions later, the same stock ripped higher — this time on a story the market is willing to pay for.

**The catalysts:** Tesla launched its driverless robotaxi service in Miami on July 3, making Florida the third state (after Texas and California) with live operations. That turned an abstract autonomy promise into a visible, expanding commercial footprint. Layered on top: a company executive teased that "cool news" about scaling at Giga Texas is coming July 7, which the market read as a signal that Cybercab manufacturing capacity is ramping toward a mass robotaxi rollout. The combination — live robotaxi expansion, the delivery-volume floor, and an imminent manufacturing catalyst — flipped sentiment from "sell the low-quality beat" to "buy the autonomy inflection."

### Fundamental Context

The delivery print (480,126) establishes a volume floor, but the July 22 earnings report is the real verdict: it will reveal whether Tesla moved that volume by *sacrificing margin*. The robotaxi story is a genuine optionality re-rating — if Miami scales and Giga Texas confirms Cybercab capacity, the market will increasingly value Tesla as an autonomy/AI platform rather than an auto OEM, which is the entire bull case for a $600 handle. The risk is equally clear: this is a stock re-rating on narrative ahead of a margin print, and a soft July 22 gross margin could turn Monday's euphoria into another "decompose-the-beat" fade. Own the story, but respect the $395 line and the margin date.

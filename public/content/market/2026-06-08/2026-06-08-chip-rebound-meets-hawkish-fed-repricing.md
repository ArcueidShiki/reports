## Market Narrative

Monday delivered a textbook relief rally that ultimately ran out of breath. After Friday's brutal session — when the Nasdaq Composite fell 4% and the semiconductor complex shed roughly $1 trillion in value on a blowout May jobs report — US equities came in firm. The S&P 500 was up as much as 0.68%, the Dow rose roughly 0.7%, and the Nasdaq Composite leapt as high as 1.6% out of the gate. By the closing bell, however, much of that had drained away: the S&P 500 finished up just 0.30% at 7,405.73, the Nasdaq held a respectable +0.86% at 25,929.66, and the Dow actually slipped 0.16% to 50,786.01 as cyclicals lagged and the late-day bid faded. The fade itself is the story — a market that wanted to buy the dip but could not fully shake the macro cloud hanging over it.

That cloud is a hawkish repricing of the Federal Reserve. Friday's May nonfarm payrolls printed +172,000 against a consensus near 85,000 — roughly double expectations — with the unemployment rate holding at 4.3%. The labor market's refusal to crack has flipped the policy conversation on its head: rather than debating how many cuts the Fed will deliver in 2026, traders are now pricing in the genuine possibility of a rate *hike*. CME FedWatch odds of at least one hike by year-end jumped to roughly 70%. The 2-year Treasury yield, the maturity most sensitive to policy, sat near 4.16% after a ~10bp Friday surge, while the 10-year hovered around 4.54%. With a fresh CPI report due Wednesday and oil prices flaring on renewed Middle East violence, the bond market is in no mood to let equities run.

Underneath the index-level calm, the composition of Monday's move was sharply bifurcated. The day's leadership came entirely from semiconductors and a handful of idiosyncratic winners, while the rest of mega-cap tech actually fell. Intel surged 11.1% to $110.19 on a report that Google had placed an order for three million of its in-house Tensor Processing Units. Micron jumped 9.6% to $947.24, Marvell leapt 9.8% to $289.40 on news of its imminent S&P 500 inclusion, and Nvidia added 1.7% to $208.58 after CEO Jensen Huang publicly called the sell-off a buying opportunity. Tesla rallied 4.6% to $409.10. But Apple fell 1.9% to $301.37 as its long-awaited Siri AI reveal at WWDC underwhelmed, and Microsoft (−1.2%), Alphabet (−1.1%), Meta (−1.3%), and Amazon (−0.3%) all closed lower. In other words: this was a chip-led bounce, not a broad-based recovery, and the breadth told you the rotation out of crowded AI names from last week had not fully reversed — it had merely paused.

The geopolitical backdrop added a layer of genuine tension. Over the weekend and into Monday, Iran fired missiles at Israel for the first time since April — in retaliation for Israeli strikes on Beirut — and Israel struck back, even as President Trump publicly urged both sides to "immediately stop shooting." On the 100th day of the broader US-Iran conflict, Brent crude spiked as much as 4% toward $98 a barrel and WTI neared $95 before both eased back as Iran said it had ended its latest volley and Trump spoke by phone with Israeli PM Netanyahu. WTI ultimately settled just above $91. For the equity market, the remarkable through-line of this entire war has been that AI optimism has consistently outweighed the war's drag — indexes powered to records through much of the spring despite elevated oil — but Monday was a reminder that the energy channel remains a live inflation risk into Wednesday's CPI.

## Macro & Economic Data

**The dominant data point is Friday's, still reverberating Monday: May Nonfarm Payrolls +172,000 vs. ~85,000 consensus, unemployment 4.3% (unchanged).** This is the print that broke the rate-cut narrative. A labor market adding roughly double the expected number of jobs, with wage growth still running above the ~3% pace the Fed views as consistent with 2% inflation, removes the Fed's justification to ease — and raises the specter of a hike if inflation re-accelerates. That is precisely why Monday's equity bounce was capped: the macro tide is running against multiple expansion.

**New York Fed Survey of Consumer Expectations (released June 8).** A growing share of households in the May survey reported their financial situation as worse than a year ago — a soft-data crack beneath the strong payrolls headline. It feeds the bifurcation thesis: a resilient labor market in aggregate, but consumers feeling real wage erosion at the margin (energy-driven inflation against ~3.4% wage growth). This is a leading indicator worth tracking into the back half of 2026.

**The week's pivotal release is Wednesday's May CPI.** With oil flaring on Middle East violence, the market is hyper-focused on whether higher energy prices are bleeding into core goods and services. A hot core print would cement the hike pricing; a benign one could let the relief rally extend. PPI follows later in the week.

Intel and Micron traded heavy on genuine fundamental catalysts (the Google TPU order, S&P inclusion flows for Marvell), which is real institutional money. But the broad-tape bounce in NVDA on lighter volume looks more like short-covering and tactical dip-buying than committed accumulation. Bullish confirmation would require NVDA to reclaim $215–$216 on *expanding* volume.

### Options Positioning
With NVDA pinned near $208 and June monthly expiry approaching (June 19), max-pain open-interest concentration sits in the ~$205–$210 zone — consistent with Monday's close and giving market makers an incentive to keep the stock range-bound into expiry. ### Fundamental Context
The structural bull case is intact and arguably reinforced Monday: Nvidia's new multi-year memory agreement with SK Hynix locks in HBM supply for next-gen architectures, and the Google–Intel TPU report signals hyperscaler AI capex is broadening, not contracting. Jensen Huang's "we're at the beginning of it… you can buy at a discount" framing is self-interested but directionally consistent with the order book. The risk is not demand — it is the discount rate. At a hawkish-Fed-driven higher-for-longer (or higher-from-here) rate regime, the present value of NVDA's long-duration earnings compresses regardless of how strong the fundamentals are. That is the tension every AI-chip holder now faces.

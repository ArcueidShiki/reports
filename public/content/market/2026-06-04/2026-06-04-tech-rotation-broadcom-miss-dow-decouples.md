## Market Narrative

Thursday's session delivered one of the sharpest intraday divergences of the young summer rally, as the Dow Jones Industrial Average climbed over 500 points while the Nasdaq sank under the weight of a Broadcom-led semiconductor selloff. The divergence was stark and deliberate: money rotated out of high-multiple chip and AI infrastructure plays and into the cyclical and defensive names that populate the blue-chip Dow index. The rotation accelerated in morning trading as investors digested Broadcom's (AVGO) disappointing Q2 fiscal 2026 earnings—reported after Wednesday's close—which revealed a modest revenue miss and a guidance framework that failed to match the explosive AI-fueled expectations Wall Street had priced in.

The geopolitical backdrop added a further layer of complexity. The U.S.-Iran conflict, now entering its second month of active strikes and counter-strikes, remained unresolved despite signals of a temporary two-month truce framework. The Republican-led House voted 215-208 to pass a war powers resolution seeking to limit President Trump's military authority against Iran—a remarkable bipartisan rebuke that underscored the political tension building in Washington. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi stated there had been "no significant progress" in negotiations while Trump said talks had gone "very well," leaving markets caught between diplomatic optimism and operational reality. Oil prices eased on hopes for Strait of Hormuz normalization, helping transport and manufacturing names that had been pressured by elevated crude.

The real story under the surface was a crypto meltdown that has now accelerated to alarming speed. Bitcoin broke below $64,000 and is now trading near $63,000—roughly 50% below its all-time high of $128,198 set in October 2025. An 11-day consecutive streak of spot ETF outflows totaling $3.45 billion, a rotation of retail and institutional capital into the upcoming SpaceX IPO and AI infrastructure names, and a persistently hawkish Fed have combined to drain speculative liquidity from the crypto complex. This matters as a macro signal: when Bitcoin falls this aggressively, it typically signals a risk-appetite reset that eventually reaches equities.

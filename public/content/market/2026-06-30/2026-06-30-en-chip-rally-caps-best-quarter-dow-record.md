## Market Narrative

Wall Street closed the book on a remarkable second quarter the way it spent most of it: with semiconductors doing the heavy lifting. On the final session of Q2 and the first half of 2026, the chip complex extended Monday's furious rebound, sending the Nasdaq Composite up roughly 1.5% to 26,213.82 and lifting the S&P 500 about 0.8% to the cusp of 7,500. The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose around 0.3% to a fresh record near 52,339 — its second straight record close after vaulting above 52,000 for the first time on Monday, when Alphabet made its debut as a Dow component. The standout single name was Advanced Micro Devices, which surged 8.15% to $583.47 and crystallized the day's message: the AI hardware trade, far from exhausted, just printed another leg higher into quarter-end.

Step back and the quarter's scoreboard is historic. The S&P 500 booked its best quarter since 2020 (on the order of +13.5% for Q2), the Nasdaq logged its best quarter in six years, and the Dow notched its strongest quarter since 2022. Beneath the surface, the dispersion was even more striking: the technology sector rose roughly 28% on the quarter and chip stocks climbed about 105% over the trailing six months, while the small-cap Russell 2000 closed out its best first half since 1991 with a gain near 22%. The "everything is the AI capex cycle" tape that defined the spring survived a brutal early-June drawdown — a 10% single-day collapse in the PHLX Semiconductor Index on June 5, a five-session Nasdaq losing streak into June 26 — and came out the other side at record highs. Resilience, not fragility, was the verdict of the quarter.

Tuesday's gains, however, were not just about silicon. Three macro and policy currents reinforced the risk-on bid. First, the Supreme Court ruled to block the administration's attempt to remove a Federal Reserve governor, leaving the central bank's independence intact "for now" — a meaningful relief for a bond market that had begun to price a politicization premium into the long end. Second, oil continued to slide as US-Iran peace talks resumed in Doha and flows through the Strait of Hormuz recovered faster than expected; the narrative has flipped from fear of shortage to warnings of a glut, and Morgan Stanley cut its crude forecasts. Third — and increasingly the market's nagging worry — the US dollar kept grinding higher, pushing the yen to a 40-year low and prompting HSBC to warn the greenback's rally could turn "explosive" if the Fed signals it is prepared to tighten. A strong dollar plus a hawkish Fed is the one combination that could eventually bite the very mega-cap multinationals leading this rally.

The day's lone fly in the ointment arrived after the bell. Nike delivered a headline beat on its fiscal Q4 but the quality of that beat was immediately questioned, and the shares slipped 3–4% in extended trading. With Constellation Brands also reporting after the close and Thursday's pulled-forward June jobs report looming, the market enters the holiday-shortened week riding records but leaning on a single sector and a benign-but-fragile macro backdrop.

## Sector & Theme Breakdown

**Semiconductors — the engine, again.** AMD was the marquee mover, up 8.15% to $583.47 on heavy volume, leading a broad chip advance that propelled the Nasdaq's outperformance. The cohort's six-month surge of roughly 105% frames just how dominant this trade has been. Nvidia, which spent late last week struggling to hold the psychologically important $200 level and posted its worst week in more than a year, firmed modestly to trade around $200–203. The internal "check-writers vs. check-receivers" divergence remained the cleanest expression of the theme: over the trailing three months, hyperscaler AI buyers (Alphabet, Microsoft, Meta) are up only about 10% combined, while the chipmakers they fund (Micron, Nvidia) are up roughly 60%. Capital is still rewarding the suppliers of compute over the spenders on compute.

**Space & defense-tech.** The space complex stayed hot following Monday's news that Rocket Lab agreed to acquire Iridium Communications for approximately $8 billion; Rocket Lab jumped more than 11% on the announcement and Iridium climbed 22%. SpaceX (SPCX) is set to join the Nasdaq-100 before the open on Tuesday, July 7, a mechanical flow event that index-tracking products will have to fund. The read-through to RKLB, LUNR and ASTS remains positive as public-market appetite for space exposure broadens.

**Software, cloud & crypto-adjacent.** Palantir extended its recovery within a volatile month, supported by its Maven AI "program of record" status with the Pentagon. CoreWeave — newly added to the Nasdaq-100 on June 22 — continued to ride the neocloud GPU-rental thesis. The conspicuous laggard was Circle Internet Group (CRCL), which tumbled about 16.7% as the stablecoin-issuer's post-IPO froth deflated alongside a weak Bitcoin tape.

**Energy, power & commodities.** Lower oil pressured energy names even as it boosted the broad tape. WTI sat near $70 and Brent below $74, both poised for quarterly losses on the order of 20–23%. Gold extended its slide to roughly $4,030/oz, near an eight-month low and on track for its worst quarter in decades as the soaring dollar and "no-cut" Fed sapped the metal's appeal. Bitcoin slipped below $60,000 to around $58,500–59,900, its weakest since 2024 and roughly 50% off its $126k all-time high — a notable risk-appetite divergence on an otherwise green equity day.

## Macro & Economic Data

**JOLTS job openings (May): 7.594 million, vs. ~7.3 million expected and 7.585 million in April (revised).** The standout upside surprise. Labor demand remains firmer than forecast even as the hiring rate stays subdued — the lingering "no-hire, no-fire" equilibrium. For a Fed whose hawks are looking for any reason to keep the option of a rate *hike* alive, a hot openings print is precisely the wrong kind of strength: it keeps wage-driven services inflation in play and removes urgency to ease. The data set the table for Thursday's June payrolls.

**Conference Board Consumer Confidence (June): 91.2, up 0.6 from a downwardly revised 90.6 in May (1985=100).** A marginal improvement that confirms the consumer is stabilizing at a historically soft level rather than rolling over — consistent with the University of Michigan's final June sentiment reading of 49.5 (up from May's record-low 44.8), helped by moderating gasoline prices.

**Chicago PMI (June): 56.7, down from 62.7 in May.** Still in expansion above 50, but a sharp six-point give-back from May's four-year high. The cooldown previews Wednesday's national ISM Manufacturing PMI and tempers the "industrial reacceleration" narrative that has underpinned the rotation into industrials and materials.

The net macro read: growth and the consumer are intact, labor demand is firmer than expected, and inflation has nowhere obvious to fall quickly. That is a "no-landing, sticky-inflation" cocktail — supportive of earnings, but the worst possible cover for anyone hoping the Fed will cut.

## Fed, Rates & Credit

The Federal Reserve under Chair Kevin Warsh held the funds rate at 3.50%–3.75% at its June 17 meeting with a hawkish tone, and nothing on June 30 changed the reaction function. The 10-year Treasury yield held around 4.38%, while the 2-year sat just above 4.0% — still above the top of the Fed's own target range, the bond market's way of saying the next move is more likely a hike than a cut. The 2s10s spread held a modestly positive ~30 basis points. Rate-cut pricing has all but evaporated: markets now treat July as a near-certain hold and September as the first genuinely "live" meeting, where the debate is hike-versus-hold rather than cut-versus-hold.

Two credit-relevant items stood out. The Supreme Court's decision to protect Fed independence removes — at least for now — a tail risk that had threatened to steepen the curve via a credibility premium. And US margin debt hit a record $1.42 trillion in May, a reminder that the leverage underpinning this rally is historically extended; early June's chip-led deleveraging showed how quickly that can unwind. Fed Chair Warsh speaks at a European forum on Wednesday — the next opportunity for the "explosive dollar" scenario to be armed or defused.

## Geopolitical & Policy

**US-Iran talks → oil → everything.** The resumption of peace talks in Doha, combined with faster-than-expected normalization of Strait of Hormuz tanker flows, drove crude lower and flipped the energy narrative from shortage to glut. Transmission mechanism: lower oil → cooler headline inflation expectations → relief in the front end of the curve → lower discount rate for long-duration equities → bid for tech. This is the "dove-path-by-default" channel — the data, not the Fed, doing the easing — and it remains the single most important macro lever for the equity tape.

**The dollar wrecking ball.** The relentless dollar advance — yen at a 40-year low, intervention chatter from Tokyo, HSBC's "explosive" warning — is the counterweight. A surging dollar tightens global financial conditions, pressures emerging markets, and erodes the foreign earnings of the US multinationals leading this rally. If the Fed validates the move by leaning hawkish, the dollar could overshoot and become a self-reinforcing headwind.

**The IEEPA tariff thread.** Nike's tariff-recovery benefit is a live signal that the legal and policy status of IEEPA-based tariffs is in flux, with companies now booking *expected refunds*. Watch for similar one-off benefits (and the cash-flow timing behind them) across the consumer and industrial complex this earnings season.

### Fundamental Context

The technical melt-up rests on a fundamental engine that is still firing: AI accelerator demand, a six-month chip-sector gain near 105%, and the "check-receivers up 60% vs. check-writers up 10%" divergence that says the supply chain captures the economics of the buildout. The risk is not the demand — it is the *discount rate*. With the 2-year above 4%, the next Fed move biased toward a hike, and the dollar surging, the threat to the group is a higher discount rate compressing the multiple even if earnings keep climbing. That is the tension to monitor: a chip melt-up powered by an AI-capex numerator, fighting a macro denominator that is quietly getting larger.
